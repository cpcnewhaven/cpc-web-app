The essentials

Our Beliefs

Five convictions that shape CPC's theology and life together

1. Scripture. We are committed to the Old and New Testament Scriptures as our only rule of faith and practice. Our Christian faith and practice is grounded in God's works of redemption in history as explained through inspired Scripture. We believe that all things necessary for salvation are sufficiently revealed by the Holy Spirit speaking through the Scriptures.

Why it matters: It keeps us anchored in God's word rather than human opinions, trends, or private revelations.

In plain language

  • Scripture is our final authority.
  • We want the whole counsel of God, not a selective message.
  • The Bible shapes both doctrine and daily life.
Grace

Teachings of Grace

Historic Reformed confessions, received with humility

We are committed to the teachings of grace as summarized in the historic Westminster Confession of Faith. Church unity is predicated upon a consensus about what the Scriptures principally teach. This means that we are accepted by God, from beginning to end, not by our own works or attempts to perform for God, but by faith alone in the perfect and sufficient work of Christ on our behalf.

Why it matters: Grace keeps the church honest, joyful, and grounded in what God has done rather than what we can produce.

Takeaway

  • God is sovereign in salvation.
  • Grace is the beginning, middle, and end of the Christian life.
  • Confession helps us stay connected to the church across generations.
Worship

God-Centered Worship

Christ present through Word, sacrament, and prayer

We are committed to God-centered worship designed after a covenantal pattern, where Christ promises to be present through Word and Sacrament. In every period of redemptive history, God has chosen to be with his people through word, sacrament, and prayer. Our weekly worship is designed to follow that gospel pattern and to welcome believers and seekers alike.

Why it matters: Worship is where the gospel becomes embodied in the gathered life of the church.

What this means

  • Word and sacrament stay central.
  • Worship is reverent, joyful, and accessible.
  • The service shapes our imagination as a church.
Church and mission

Church Planting and a Multi-Cultural Witness

The church as a living sign of Christ's kingdom

We are committed to being a multi-cultural church and church planting movement. We believe that the church, the household of God, is an essential element of the gospel into which people of every tribe and nation are welcomed. Our aim is not just to grow, but to grow in a way that extends Christ's witness into New Haven and beyond.

Why it matters: The gospel is for every culture, and the church should make that visible.

Mission shape

  • We want a church that is open and inviting.
  • We want to plant churches that reach different cultures.
  • We want mission to feel local, not abstract.
Calling

A High View of Christian Calling

Family, church, and public service as spheres of faithfulness

We are committed to a high view of Christian calling as reflected in the vocational spheres of family, church, and public service. God has given us roles that reflect his own sovereign design to accomplish his work in the world. We desire to help people find a balance that is unique to their individual callings and spheres of service.

Why it matters: The Christian life is not limited to Sunday, and our whole lives belong to God.

Everyday faithfulness

  • Faith shapes vocation, family, and public life.
  • We want doctrine to be lived, not just discussed.
  • All of life can be offered as thankful worship.
Adult studies

What We Believe: Knowing and Loving Our Doctrines

A school of discipleship class that walks through the historic Christian faith

We’re called to not just know about God, but to know God personally. This class walks through the historic Christian beliefs we hold to at CPC, grappling with why they matter, where they come from, and how they shape our love for the Lord and his Word. It is more than a lecture. It is a space to discuss how theology informs our spiritual, emotional, and relational health.

Sundays 9/17/23 – 5/19/24 at 9:30am in the Fellowship Hall. No class on Thanksgiving weekend, winter recess, or Easter Sunday.

Why this class exists

  • To ground believers in the historic faith.
  • To equip leaders and teachers at CPC.
  • To connect doctrine with daily discipleship.
